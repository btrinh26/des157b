Annotated Bibliography

LNASA. The Effects of Climate Change. NASA, https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/effects/. Accessed 19 May 2025.

This article from NASA goes over the full effects of climate change caused by our excessive greenhouse gas emissions. Some effects include rising sea levels, extreme weather events, and ecosystem disruption. This article feels useful to me, because NASA feels like an extremely credible source. The references at the footer also feel like useful sources to draw upon. This article is also useful in seeing not only the short term effects like longer wildfire seasons and rising sea levels, but also the long term effects like melting ice caps and stronger hurricanes. It says finally that we need strategies of adaptation and mitigation to start right now to avoid damage.

Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). Sixth Assessment Report: Working Group I – The Physical Science Basis – Press Release. IPCC, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/resources/press. Accessed 19 May 2025.

This source says that humans are almost certainly responsible for the climate change that is happening right now. Climate change is caused mainly by greenhouse gases that humans have been emitting at an alarming rate since the industrial revolution. This source feels really credible too, since it is backed and authored by hundreds of climate scientists. It provides findings on climate change (temperature increase), atmospheric concentration trends (distributions of molecules in the atmosphere), and feedback loops (the rate of our warming is increasing). I found it helpful not just for statistics, but it is urgent in wanting to reduce emissions.

United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). The Paris Agreement. United Nations, https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement. Accessed 19 May 2025.

I knew that the US withdrew from the Paris agreement under the Trump Administration. It is a set of global climate rules adopted in 2015 that aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C. 2°C refers to the change in temperature since the Industrial Revolution; 2°C would be the point where there would be catastrophic and irreversible damage to the Earth. Right now, the average global temperature has increased by 1.6°C since the industrial revolution and it is only increasing. It is a treaty signed by nearly every country (not the US anymore) in the world.The UNFCCC says that although some progress has been made, the current promises from countries are not enough to reach the agreement’s goals. This shows why my project needs to highlight the importance of stronger climate action and responsibility.
